========================================

        weather NAME hooks added and host-verified 2026-09-07, NOT yet seen on
        screen. The legacy
        build had FIVE GROUPS WORKING IN GAME (2026-09-04) at the shipped defaults:
        sandstorm, ash fall, blizzard, embers, fog.
        FIREFLIES, LEAVES, BLOOD RAIN and DARKNESS added after that and NOT
        yet seen on screen. Blood rain and darkness reuse proven machinery
        (the streak engine turned upright, and the fog tint with another
        colour); fireflies and leaves are the first users of hover, sway and
        pulse, so they are where to look first if something is wrong. Sandstorm, ash fall, blizzard and embers were right first
        try. Fog took four goes -- a dither (wrong on screen), a flat
        mid-grey blend (correct but nearly invisible), a near-white graded
        blend (right, but banded at 8 bands), and the same at 128 bands,
        which is what ships. Every wrong version is written up below; the
        reasons generalise to anything that tints the screen.

        Banding at 128 bands is reported as "much less apparent" rather than
        gone. If it ever needs to be chased further, the remaining lever is
        not more bands -- the step is already at the client's own
        quantisation -- but an ordered dither on the band index, which trades
        the row-pointer optimisation for dispersing the boundary. Nobody has
        asked for that, and it may just be the 16-bit gradient itself.

The managed build is not deployed pending the atomic core-only autoload

retains its specialised dynamic caller discovery and frozen patch writer. Its
real-DLL fixture exercises the icon invariant, two SetWeather callers, draw

What it does
------------

The client draws rain, snow, clouds and thunder from code, one particle group
per bit of the weather code the server sends in 0x00030015. Bits 4 upward are
unused. This mod draws five more groups from them:

    bit  4 (0x0010)  Sandstorm   near-horizontal sand streaks
    bit  5 (0x0020)  Ash fall    slow grey flakes with an occasional ember
    bit  6 (0x0040)  Blizzard    wind-driven snow, steeper and faster than sand
    bit  7 (0x0080)  Embers      motes drifting UPWARD, for lava maps
    bit  8 (0x0100)  Fog         a graded haze over the whole scene
    bit  9 (0x0200)  Fireflies   hovering motes that blink independently
    bit 10 (0x0400)  Leaves      green motes that swing as they fall
    bit 11 (0x0800)  Blood rain  the streak engine turned upright, in red
    bit 12 (0x1000)  Darkness    tints the scene toward night

All of them compose with the stock four, because the wire code is a bitmask:
0x14 is "sandstorm and clouds" and is a legal code. Two combinations are worth
knowing:

    Blizzard | LightSnow (0x0042)  a stock client still sees falling snow where
                                   a patched one sees a storm -- the best kind
                                   of new weather, because it degrades
    Embers | AshFall | Fog (0x01a0) an eruption
    Fireflies | Darkness (0x1200)  dusk in a forest; the tint is what makes
                                   the fireflies read as light
    BloodRain | Darkness (0x1800)  event dressing that needs no new art

The server half is live (mw-tools, 2026-09-04): all five codes exist on the
Weather enum, encodeWeatherCode passes bits 4-8 through (weatherBitMask is
0x1ff), and !weather sandstorm / ashfall / blizzard / embers / fog reach the
map. Ambient weather deliberately does NOT pick any of them yet -- see
"Turning it on" below.


Why bits 4 and 5 are safe
-------------------------

SetWeather (0x46f950) builds its group mask from bits 0-3 only:

    0046f966  xor edx, edx
    0046f968  test al, 1        -> edx |= 1
    ...
    0046f97f  test al, 8        -> edx |= 8
    0046f988  test eax, 0x1000000   -> ecx = 1
    0046f994  test eax, 0x2000000   -> ecx = 2
    0046f9a0  push ecx / push 1 / push edx / call WeatherApplyGroups

Everything above bit 3 (except the two intensity bits) is read and dropped. An
unpatched client therefore ignores a sandstorm code instead of misbehaving,
which is what makes a mixed patched/unpatched population work with no
server-side branching.


The hooks
---------

Two carry the effects (SetWeather and WeatherDraw). Three are cosmetic and
optional: the HUD icon picker's call site, and the single call site of each of
the two weather-name builders. Each cosmetic one stands itself down on its own
without touching the effects.

Every one of them is a CALL-SITE patch: five bytes of displacement at a call,
with the callee left byte for byte untouched. No prologue is relocated and no
executable memory is allocated, so there is no trampoline to get wrong and
nothing to unmap on eject.

1. Every `call SetWeather` site -> wx_setweather_detour.

   Found at runtime by walking .text for an E8 whose rel32 lands exactly on
   the resolved SetWeather. The stock client has four:

       0x418bf5   the thunk the 0x00030015 packet handler calls through
       0x448f88   game-state transition re-apply
       0x4493c0
       0x46e2e5   map-load re-apply, from the stored block at 0x740f1c

   The detour records our bits and the intensity, then calls the real
   function. It has to be here: this is the last place the full dword exists.

2. The single `call WeatherDraw` site at 0x4491b9 -> wx_draw_detour.

   Resolved as RenderCallSite + 0x23. That offset is PAST the end of the
   matched pattern, so install verifies two things before writing: the byte is
   E8, and its current target is the WeatherDraw the scan resolved separately.

   The site, not the function, on purpose. It sits inside the block guarded by
   `cmp byte [0x5e1540], bl` at 0x4491aa -- the client's own "draw weather at
   all" gate -- so hooking there inherits that gate for free.


Where our state lives, and why not in the client's word
-------------------------------------------------------

Our two bits are held in the DLL (g_mask), NOT OR-ed into the client's active
mask at 0x7decb0.

That is not tidiness. The HUD weather-icon picker at 0x46f9b0 is an equality
chain over that word with no default:

    mask 0 -> early return       8 -> 1      4 -> 2      1 -> 3      2 -> 4
    anything containing snow -> 5
    any other combination containing rain -> 6
    the rest (thunder+clouds) -> 7

A bit it has no case for falls off the end to `not al & 1`, so bit 4 alone
would come out as icon 7 and relabel a sandstorm as thunder and clouds, and a
sandstorm during rain would downgrade rain's icon 3 to icon 6. Keeping our
bits out of that word means the stock icon stays correct for the stock four
and costs nothing.

That still stands. The icon is now handled by hooking the PICKER instead --
see below -- which is the same decision from the other end: the client's mask
word stays exactly as the client wrote it, and we answer the icon question
ourselves.


The HUD weather icon
--------------------

A 32x32 RAYS IMG256 sprite blitted at (741, 4), top right, out of
system\weather.mda ([0x703204], loaded at 0x45cd1c). 0x46f9b0 picks the index,
0x4411c6 formats it into "MainCommand%d" and draws it.

CORRECTION TO AN EARLIER NOTE IN THIS FILE: the stock chain does NOT "return
early and draw nothing" for a mask of 0. It returns eax = 0 and MainCommand0
IS drawn -- it is the clear-sky sun. Index 0 is taken.

    0 clear   1 thunder   2 clouds   3 rain   4 snow
    5 any combination with snow   6 any other with rain   7 thunder + clouds

Ours take 8 upward in bit order, so a group's index is 4 + its bit number:

    8 sandstorm   9 ash   10 blizzard   11 embers   12 fog
    13 fireflies  14 leaves  15 blood rain  16 darkness

g_icon_map is the authority on that, and it is in PRIORITY order rather than
numeric order: the first running group in the list names the weather.
Precipitation and storms come first because that is what a player would call
the weather they are standing in, and the tints and ambient motes come last --
so fireflies+darkness shows fireflies, and sandstorm+fog shows the sandstorm.
Ours outrank the client's four outright: a sandstorm blowing through cloud is
a sandstorm, and the stock chain would have called it clouds.

The hook is the picker's single call site, found by walking .text for an E8
that lands on it, exactly as SetWeather's four are. With none of our groups
running the detour calls the original, so the stock four keep the icons they
always had -- which is what makes it safe to leave installed.

IT IS SELF-CHECKING, AND THE PROBE IS LAZY. The detour asks the bank for
MainCommand16 by name on its FIRST CALL, not at install (0x466b30,
`find(name, bank)` -- NOT (bank, name); the bank is arg2 and is an INDEX
scaled by 0x144 into the table at 0x743b1c, which is why AniLoad writes -1
into the global on failure, and why a negative index must never reach the
lookup). If the artwork is absent the detour falls through to the client's own
picker forever, because asking for a MainCommand that is not in the bank makes
the icon silently vanish, and a wrong icon beats a missing one.

The first build probed at install and the new icons never appeared while the
stock ones kept working. The autoload cave calls LoadLibraryA from the CRT
ENTRY POINT, so the mod installs long before the client loads
system\weather.mda at 0x45cd1c: the probe asked an empty bank, was told no,
and latched the feature off for the session. The colour converter is deferred
to the first frame for exactly this reason and the icon probe simply was not.

ANY resource this mod wants to ask the client about has to be asked for late.
Install time is before the game has loaded anything.

[Weather] Icons=0 turns the hook off.

Authoring notes, from the loader (0x466270):
  - Binding is BY NAME at both levels, so file order does not matter. The
    animation lookup is a linear _strcmpi search, and frames reference files
    by name string. That is why the stock files are named 000.9, 001.2,
    002.3 ... 007.8 and it still works.
  - Matching is CASE-INSENSITIVE.
  - Names must be UNDER 19 CHARACTERS -- both animation names and frame file
    names are checked with `cmp eax, 0x13`. Over that, the client pops "MDA
    filename too long, please reinstall client or contact a GM!" AND SKIPS THE
    NAME COPY, leaving an entry that exists but can never be found.
  - The stock sprites are 32x32, centre (384, 284), about 2.7 KB each.


The weather's NAME -- tooltip and status line
--------------------------------------------

Added 2026-09-07. The icon was only half the problem: the WORDS still said
Sunny for all nine groups.

CORRECTION TO THE SPEC. weather-effects.md said "There is no tooltip" and
that naming a new weather on screen had to be server-side text. Both halves
were wrong, and the way they were wrong generalises: the search was for a
hit-test on the icon, and there genuinely is none. The tooltip is not the
icon's. It is the HUD's DEFAULT hover tooltip, drawn by 0x441090 when the
cursor has been still for 0x3E8 ms ([0x671738] vs [0x702980], reset on any
mouse move at 0x4419c0), [0x702860] is -1, and nothing in the rect table at
0x7036e0 claimed the cursor first. Looking for the hit-test that names the
weather was looking for a thing that does not exist.

There are TWO name builders, both `void __cdecl(char *dest)`:

    0x46eae0   "%s - %s Wind" (0x57e034)                    the tooltip
               one call site, 0x441b86, in the HUD draw 0x441090
    0x46e820   "#Y%s#N: #Y%s#N and #Y%s#N Wind " (0x57dfb0) the status line
               one call site, 0x46ee14, in WeatherTick

Both build the weather word by testing the CLIENT's mask at 0x7decb0 -- which
is exactly where our bits deliberately are not -- and both end with "if what I
built is <= 1 char, write Sunny (0x57dfd0) over it". So the Sunny branch is
the one that always ran for us. The bug was a consequence of the mask decision
above, not of anything missing from the client.

THEY ARE NOT BYTE-IDENTICAL CLONES, which the spec also claimed. They share
their first 0xb4 bytes and then diverge in instruction scheduling. So no
prologue pattern separates them -- and an operand offset measured in one is
NOT valid in the other, which is why the intensity words below are read out of
the tooltip builder only.

Both are reached through their call sites (Weather_TooltipCallSite,
Weather_StatusCallSite, both optional=1), each of which yields the site to
patch and, through its E8, the builder to call.

.rdata space was never the obstacle either. Each detour calls the original
into a scratch buffer and replaces ONLY the weather field, splicing on the
literal middle of the client's own format string:

    tooltip      everything before the first " - "
    status line  between "#N: #Y" and "#N and #Y"

Neither a map name nor a weather word can contain those. The wind word, the
map name and the colour markup stay the client's, untranslated. Anything
unexpected copies the original's own output through unchanged and logs why: a
stale Sunny is what happens today and beats a mangled line.

The NAME comes from g_icon_map, same rows and same first-match walk as the
icon, so the glyph and the words can never name different groups. Blood Rain,
Sandstorm, Blizzard, Ash Fall, Embers, Falling Leaves, Fog, Fireflies,
Darkness.

The "Light "/"Medium "/"Heavy " prefix is read out of the TOOLTIP builder's
body rather than shipped as our own literals, so it matches whatever the
client's own rain and snow say:

    +0xA6  8B 35  mov esi, [abs32]   "Light "
    +0xD8  A1     mov eax, [abs32]   "Heavy "
    +0xF8  A1     mov eax, [abs32]   "Medium "

Each is guarded by the opcode that must be there AND by a VirtualQuery on the
operand before it is dereferenced -- matching the opcode is not proof the
operand is an address, and on a client we have never seen those bytes could be
anything. Any read that does not check out keeps our own literal and logs it;
the feature degrades to slightly-wrong wording rather than to a crash.

NO CALLER'S BUFFER SEES ANYTHING NEW, which is what makes this safe without
asserting a bound on someone else's stack: the field we substitute is at most
"Medium " + "Falling Leaves" = 21 chars, against the stock builder's own
40-char worst case of "Medium Rain" + "Medium Snow" + "Clouds" +
"Thunderstorm". (The HUD frame has 0x3F0 unused bytes above esp+0x74 anyway,
and the tooltip box is sized 8*len+8 px and clamped to 800.)

The two sites are patched together or not at all: a tooltip saying Sandstorm
while the status line says Sunny is worse than both saying Sunny. If the
second claim is refused the first is rolled back.

[Weather] Names=0 turns both off, independently of Icons. Two separate client
surfaces, and being able to turn one off without the other is what makes a
client update bisectable.

The lifecycle fixture drives this end to end: two synthetic call sites laid
out byte for byte the way the signatures find the client's (E8 at exactly
+0x12 and +0x05, target opening A1 ?? ?? ?? ?? 81 EC A0 00 00 00), a fake
builder that produces the client's own Sunny strings, and assertions that an
idle sky is byte-identical, that a running group is named in both lines with
everything around it intact, and that shutdown puts the client's own text
back.


Drawing
-------

Rain and snow need no artwork -- they are generic clipped primitives against
the back buffer taking an arbitrary 16-bit colour -- which is the whole reason
these two effects are cheap. We use the same primitives:

    DrawLine   0x48c380   (x1, y1, x2, y2, colour, surface)   cdecl, clips
    DrawPixel  0x48c340   (x, y, colour, surface)             cdecl, clips

NOT 0x48c320. That is the raw pixel writer -- `word [rows[y] + x*2] = colour`
with no bounds check whatsoever -- and one stray coordinate there is a write
outside the surface. The spec's first draft named it; do not use it.

Colour is 16-bit but the FORMAT is not assumed. The client keeps an
RGB888 -> native16 converter in a function pointer at 0x7e6920 and builds its
own palette through it (`push 0xffffff; call [0x7e6920]` -> the white at
0x7e6940 that the rain lines use). We resolve that global, call it once on the
first frame that has it filled, and cache five colours. So the mod is correct
on 565 and 555 alike, and if video is not up yet it simply draws nothing that
frame and tries again.

Surface layout, from the primitives themselves: width at +0, height at +4, row
pointer table at +0xC. Only the two dimensions are read.


The tick
--------

There is none, and there is deliberately no worker thread. The draw detour is
the tick: particles are advanced in the same call that draws them, at the rate
the client already animates its own groups. A thread parked inside the DLL is
a thread whose code vanishes on eject.

Randomness is a private xorshift32, not rand(). The client consumes the CRT
generator on its own schedule (thunder reschedules at rand()%50+600), and a
mod pulling from the same stream perturbs client behaviour unrelated to
weather. Four bytes of state to avoid that is a good trade.

Particles are static arrays sized by WX_MAX_SAND / WX_MAX_ASH. Nothing here
allocates after install, so there is no path from a weather packet to the heap.


Two engines, four particle groups
---------------------------------

A STREAK is a fast near-horizontal line; a MOTE is a slow drifting point.
Sandstorm and blizzard are the same engine with a different parameter block,
and so are ash and embers. That is the whole reason a new weather is cheap:

    streak   wind        horizontal px/frame, and wind_var its spread
             fall_min/max vertical px/frame
             stretch     streak length as a PERCENT OF ONE FRAME'S TRAVEL

    mote     vy_min/max  SIGNED. Positive falls, negative rises, and a range
                         that STRADDLES ZERO means hover -- that is what makes
                         fireflies possible without a third engine. Embers are
                         ash with the sign flipped, and enter from the bottom
                         instead of the top because of it.
             drift       sideways wander
             sway        horizontal oscillation on the particle's own phase
             pulse       swap between two colours on that phase
             palette     the base colours, picked from uniformly
             special     one in N takes the accent colour; it is what stops a
                         field of grey reading as snow

The streak's `stretch` being a percentage of one frame's travel, rather than a
fixed length, is what let blood rain exist without new code: give a group a
small wind and a large fall and the same motion blur that draws a flat sand
streak draws a near-vertical fall of blood. The engine never learns which it
is drawing.

Sway and pulse both ride on a per-particle phase with a per-particle rate. The
rate matters: with one shared rate a field of leaves swings in unison and
reads as a single object rather than as many.

A mote group carries a PALETTE rather than two colours, and that is not
decoration. Leaves shipped as gold, brown and red -- the same warm family as
ash and embers -- and in motion three of the nine groups looked like the same
effect. They are now four greens and one gold, with the red kept as a rare
accent (1 in 16, up from 1 in 7) because a few turned leaves make the rest
read as green while many make the whole field read as fire.

Two base colours was the real limit, not the choice of them: a group needs
enough hues to read as a material rather than as a colour. WX_MAX_PALETTE is
6, and the greens were checked through the 16-bit conversion before shipping
-- 0x3E6B24 through 0x8CA83A stay distinctly green at 565, spanning
(57,105,32) to (139,170,57), so the field has depth rather than being one flat
tone.

Adding a group is a row in g_streaks[] or g_motes[], a bit in the header, and
the matching server enum entry. Nothing else.


Fog: the dither that failed, and the blend that replaced it
-----------------------------------------------------------

The first version dithered: every Nth pixel of every row overwritten with a
flat grey, on the reasoning that the client has no alpha to borrow. In game it
drew as hard diagonal lines crawling sideways across the scene. Both halves of
that were the code doing exactly what it said:

  - the start offset was (y + phase) % step, which increments once per row,
    and a set of points one apart on successive rows IS a diagonal line. A
    dispersed screen needs the offset to ALTERNATE (a Bayer matrix), not
    increment. "Offset per row" was the wrong idea, not a wrong constant.
  - the crawl was the phase advance, moving the whole pattern one pixel
    sideways every 16 frames.

Fixing the geometry would not have saved it. An opaque mid-grey written over a
bright scene reads as dark speckle however it is arranged, because overwriting
is not mixing. On the bright temple courtyard it was tested on, the "fog" was
darker than the ground it was supposed to be hazing.

The premise was the real mistake. "The client has no alpha" is true of
FillRect and irrelevant here: fog writes the row table itself, so it can read
each pixel first and mix it. It is now a real alpha blend.

Blending needs the pixel format, which is the one thing this mod had been
careful never to assume -- and it still does not have to. Feeding the client's
own RGB888 -> native16 converter pure red, green and blue hands back the three
channel masks directly:

    conv(0xFF0000) -> 0xF800   conv(0x00FF00) -> 0x07E0   conv(0x0000FF) -> 0x001F

so the layout is measured at runtime and 565 and 555 both work. The masks are
logged at startup; if they ever come back zero or wider than the lookup table,
draw_fog stands itself down rather than taking a pixel apart with a guess.

Cost is three lookups and two ORs per pixel. Each channel gets a 64-entry
table -- unpack, mix toward the fog colour, repack, all collapsed into one
indexed read -- rebuilt only when the alpha changes. 800x600 is 480,000
iterations a frame, roughly 2% of a frame against FrameSync's 30 ms floor.

Alpha is 25/40/55 percent for light/medium/heavy, or pin it with FogAlpha.
Above 90 the scene is gone rather than fogged, so the config clamps.

Fog draws FIRST, before the particles, so a heavy fog does not swallow them.


Two things the first blend got wrong
------------------------------------

The blend worked on the first try but read as almost nothing on a bright map.
Both causes were choices, not bugs:

1. THE FOG COLOUR WAS MID-GREY (0xC8C8C8). Blending toward mid-grey darkens
   every highlight while lifting every shadow -- contrast compression, which
   the eye reads as the colour draining out of the scene rather than as
   weather. Measured at 25%, a highlight went 247 -> 230 and bright stone
   214 -> 206: the fog was making the bright courtyard DARKER.

   It is now 0xE0E4EC, near-white with a slight blue. At the same alpha the
   highlight goes 247 -> 239 and bright stone does not move at all, while the
   shadow still lifts 58 -> 107. That is what haze actually does: it adds
   light, it does not average the picture toward grey.

2. IT WAS FLAT. A uniform wash over every pixel is a screen filter, not
   distance. In an isometric view UP IS AWAY -- the top of the frame is the
   far distance -- so the fog is now graded top to bottom: 135%% of the base
   alpha at the top of the screen falling to 55%% at the bottom, over eight
   horizontal bands.

   The gradient is free. Each band gets its own blend table and the row picks
   a table pointer, so the inner loop is byte for byte what it was when the
   fog was flat.

   FogGradient=0 turns it off if a map ever wants a flat wash.

3. THE GRADIENT SHIPPED WITH TOO FEW BANDS. Eight was a guess, and the seams
   were visible in game -- worst while moving, because the bands are fixed in
   screen space while the world scrolls under them, and a static horizontal
   edge is what the eye is best at finding.

   The measure that matters is the largest change in the final 5/6-bit
   channel value between two vertically adjacent pixels; anything above 1 is
   a contour. tools/bands.py computes it over every source value and every
   screen row:

       bands    a=25  a=40  a=55  a=90     tables
           8       2     3     4     7       3 KB
          32       1     2     2     2      12 KB
          64       1     1     2     2      24 KB
         128       1     1     1     1      48 KB

   128 is the first count where every alpha is down to one unit, which is the
   client's own quantisation and therefore invisible. That is what ships. Do
   not lower it without re-running tools/bands.py -- and note that raising the
   alpha ceiling would need it re-run too, since the jump scales with alpha.

Worth knowing for the next effect: all three mistakes are available to
anything that tints the screen. Pick the tint colour by what it does to
highlights, grade it by screen height unless there is a reason not to, and
size the quantisation of that gradient by measuring the adjacent-pixel step
rather than by picking a round number.


Gusting is not a weather
------------------------

It is how wind behaves, so it is a property of every streak group rather than
a bit the server can set. Density swings around its base value on a slow cycle
(WX_GUST_PERIOD frames, one shared phase so a sandstorm and a blood rain surge
together rather than beating against each other).

It modulates the DENSITY, not the wind speed. Changing the speed of particles
already in flight makes the whole field jerk; changing how many there are
makes the storm breathe. The spawn ramp of 8 per frame does the rest -- a gust
arrives over a second or so instead of popping a hundred particles into being.

Gust=0 turns it off.


Two tints, one pass
-------------------

Fog and darkness are the same full-screen blend with different colours and
gradients, so they are two instances of wx_tint_t rather than two copies of
the code. Darkness ships FLAT: night does not thicken toward the horizon, and
a graded darkness reads as a vignette, which is a camera artefact rather than
weather.

Darkness tints toward 0x101822, not 0x000000. A pure black tint just crushes
the scene; leaving a trace of blue in it reads as night.

The lookup tables live OUTSIDE the struct, and that is not style. A struct
with any non-zero initialiser goes to .data whole, so folding 48 KB of zeroed
table into each tint put 96 KB of nothing into the DLL image and grew the file
from 125 KB to 225 KB. Held separately they are .bss and cost nothing on disk.
Watch for this if a third tint is ever added.

------

    [Addons]
    Weather=1          the one gate; 0 stands the mod down, client untouched

    [Weather]
    SandWind=14        sand speed in px/frame; blows left, always moving
    AshDrift=1         sideways wander for ash, clamped to 0-4
    BlizzardWind=18    same, for the blizzard streaks
    EmberDrift=2       sideways wander for embers, clamped to 0-4
    FogAlpha=0         fog mix percent: 0 follows intensity (25/40/55 for

    FogGradient=1      denser at the top of the screen, which is the far

    DarknessAlpha=0    as FogAlpha, for the darkness tint (30/45/62)

    Gust=35            gust depth as a percent of base density; 0 disables

    ModLogging=0       shared with the other mods


Turning it on
-------------

Three things, in this order:

1. Re-patch the client so it loads the DLL. The autoload list is baked in at
   patch time and there is no in-place edit, so it is revert-then-reapply with
   the FULL list:

       python tools/client-patch/mod_autoload.py --revert --in-place client/main.exe
       python tools/client-patch/mod_autoload.py --in-place client/main.exe \
           --dlls osmw_login.dll,osmw_chat.dll,osmw_minimap.dll,\
osmw_fightspeed.dll,osmw_weather.dll

   Cave budget: the name block is 128 bytes and five names use 124 of them. It
   fits. A SIXTH mod does not, and the fix is in mw-tools
   tools/client-patch/README.md under "Read-only slack in .rdata" -- move the
   block, do not shorten the names.

2. Re-run every mod's scan_offsets.py against the patched exe. Adding a name
